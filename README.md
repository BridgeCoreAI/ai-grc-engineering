## What this is

A structured, module-by-module engineering walkthrough of three major AI governance frameworks: NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, and the EU AI Act. Each framework is broken into complete, sequential modules explaining its structure, functions, and requirements, written to move from framework familiarity toward the ability to actually operationalize governance requirements.

## Why it exists

Most AI governance material stops at policy summary. This repository exists to work through each major framework in enough depth to support real operational application, not just familiarity, building toward the broader practice of translating governance requirements into systems that are actually operated, measured, and audited.

## What's inside

**NIST AI RMF, 8 modules, complete.**
Framework overview and stakeholders; the four core functions (Govern, Map, Measure, Manage); key concepts (trustworthy AI, lifecycle, risk types, impact); real-world application and industry use cases; governance controls; audit readiness; and GRC engineering practice. Plus four reference cards: the seven properties of trustworthy AI, AI lifecycle stages, AI risk types, and human oversight levels.

**ISO/IEC 42001, 9 modules, complete.**
Framework overview; the seven management-system clauses; Annex A controls; the certification and audit cycle; risk assessment and treatment (Clause 6); operational controls (Clause 8); performance evaluation (Clause 9); continual improvement (Clause 10); and impact assessments (Annex B).

**EU AI Act, 9 modules, complete.**
Framework overview; prohibited and high-risk AI systems; transparency and GPAI obligations; conformity assessment; governance, enforcement, and penalties; risk management (Article 9); data governance and human oversight; implementation timeline; and a cross-framework comparison.

## How it fits the broader practice

This public repository is the demonstrable, teachable layer of a larger body of work: 26 complete framework modules across three major governance frameworks, plus supporting reference material. It reflects a practice built around one governing idea, governance is not what is defined, it is what is enforced at execution.
